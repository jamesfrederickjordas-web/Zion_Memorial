Abstract\
This project focuses on developing a Cloud -Based Online Reservation and
Records Management System with integrated interactive mapping for Zion
Memorial Garden. At present, the garden relies on traditional manual
ledgers, which have led to noticeable delays in sales and burial
coordination. The goal is to replace these paper- based methods with a
secure, cloud-integrated Software as a Service (SaaS) platform that
supports online plot reservations, payment recording through Cash, Card,
GCash, and Maya, and ins tallment (amortization) plans. By using
interactive SVG map libraries ,the system will provide a visual layout
of the garden plots, enabling staff to manage and locate spaces, and
clients to view plot availability through a simple web interface.

The system's backend will be hosted on secure cloud servers so that
records are protected and remain accessible from any internet-enabled
device. By digitizing these workflows, Zion Memorial Garden can reduce
administrative overhead, minimize human error, and preserve its
historical records in a reliable digital format for years to come.
Keywords: cloud-based, interactive mapping, records management, SaaS,
plot reservation.

1.1 Background of the Study\
Zion Memorial Garden is a key provider of burial services and memorial
spaces for the local community. Managing these services involves a
constant flow of plot reservations, burial record updates, and client
data management, all of which are vital to maintaining the garden's
integrity and service quality. However, the garden still depends on a
manual ledger system for its daily transactions and record-keeping.
Relying on physical paperwork creates several bottlenecks: data
retrieval is slow, physical files are at constant risk of being lost,
and there is no clear way to visualize where plots are located without
walking the grounds or cross - referencing maps. As the garden's records
grow, this manual approach becomes increasingly difficult to manage and
more prone to mistakes. To address these problems, this study proposes a
cloud -based digital system with interactive mapping. By moving records
online and providing a visual map of the garden, the system aims to
modernize operations and make the management of Zion Memorial Garden
more efficient.

1.4 Scope and Limitations Scope:\
This study covers the development of a digital platform for Zion
Memorial Garden, focusing on online plot reservations, digital
record-keeping, payment processing (Cash, Card, GCash, and Maya),
amortization (installment) plans, and an interactive cemetery map. The
system is intended for use by both staff (for daily management) and
customers (to view availability and navigate the grounds). All data will
be stored securely in the cloud and will be accessible from any
internet-enabled device.

Limitations:\
• The system requires an active internet connection to function. • The
project does not handle full financial accounting or payroll; its
payment and amortization features are limited to recording and tracking
client transactions. • The digital map is restricted to the physical
layout data currently available from the garden.

2.0 Review of Related Literature and Studies\
Review of Related Literature (RRL)\
Cloud computing is a powerful technology for building modern records
management systems. It allows organizations to store records in a secure
central location, access them quickly from any device with an internet
connection, create automatic backups, and share information easily. Many
systems are delivered as Software as a Service (SaaS), which means the
software runs on a cloud server, and users do not need to install or
maintain it themselves. This approach reduces costs, improves
efficiency, and makes it much easier to manage growing amounts of data
compared to traditional paper-based ledgers.

This technology is especially useful for handling sensitive records like
plot reservations, burial details, and client information. It supports
fast data retrieval, reduces the risk of losing important documents, and
provides strong security features such as encryption and access
controls. However, adopting cloud systems also requires careful
attention to data privacy, internet reliability, and long-term record
preservation.

This literature is highly relevant to the proposed system. It shows how
a secure cloud- hosted SaaS platform with an intuitive front-end
interface and interactive mapping tools like SVG map libraries can
replace the current manual ledger system. The system will digitize
records, enable clear visualization of burial plots, streamline sales
and burial management, ensure high data integrity and availability from
any internet -enabled device, and help preserve important historical
records while improving overall operational workflow (Shi et al., 2021).

Review of Related Studies (RRS)\
A case study conducted at Woodland Hills Memorial Park in Minnesota
explored the application of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) for
cemetery management with limited resources. The study converted
handwritten records into digital formats and integrated them with GPS
-collected spatial data to construct a geodatabase. Key capabilities
demonstrated include accurate spatial visualization of burial plots,
efficient record updating and querying, pattern analysis of burials, and
improved planning for cemetery development.

The findings showed that GIS integration enhances spatial accuracy and
decision - making support while addressing the lack of clear plot
location visualization common in manual systems. Limitations noted
include the significant initial effort and time required for data
digitization and accurate GPS surveying. This study is directly
applicable to the proposed system because the interactive mapping
feature for Zion Memorial Garden can adopt similar GIS techniques for
real -time plot visualization, online plot reservations, and accurate
records management (Schmidt et al., 2020).

4.0 System Overview

4.1 Overview of the System\
The Cloud -Based Online Reservation and Records Management System with
Interactive Mapping is a comprehensive web-based Software as a Service
(SaaS) platform developed for Zion Memorial Garden. This system is
designed to replace the current paper-based manual ledger system with a
secure, cloud -hosted digital solution that is accessible from any
internet-enabled device.

The system consists of three primary functional features:\
• Online Plot Reservation : This feature enables the real -time booking,
monitoring, and management of burial plot reservations for both staff
and\
clients.\
• Digital Records Management : This feature establishes a structured and
searchable digital repository for all burial transactions and client
information.\
• Interactive Cemetery Mapping: This feature provides a visual and
interactive representation of the memorial garden's plot layout,
utilizing open -source mapping libraries. • Payment Processing: This
feature allows clients to pay through Cash, Card, GCash, or Maya, with
every transaction recorded and confirmed in the system.\
• Amortization: This feature lets clients settle a reservation through
installment plans, with an automatically computed payment schedule and
balance tracking.\
The system's backend will be hosted on a secure cloud server, which is
designed to provide high data availability, automatic backups, and
remote access. Role -based access control will differentiate the views
available to administrators, staff, and clients, thereby protecting data
privacy and strengthening operational security.

Database Schema\
The database schema includes eight relational tables: user,
memorial_space, reservation, payment, memorial_record,
maintenance_visit, staff, and support_ticket (Figure 7). Each table
captures essential data related to clients, memorials, transactions, and
administrative operations. Foreign keys maintain referential integrity
by linking reservations to users and memorial spaces, payments to
reservations, and maintenance visits to staff and spaces.

Auto-increment integer primary keys ensure unique identification and
efficient queries. A relational database model, implemented in
PostgreSQL through Supabase, is preferred because the data is structured
and its entities are interconnected, which requires the consistency and
integrity that a relational design supports.
